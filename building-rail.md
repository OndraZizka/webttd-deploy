Railways are useful when a road vehicle would carry too little, or when an industry and its customers are far apart. A
classic freight plan is a coal mine connected to a power station. Passenger trains can also link towns when you build
passenger-capable stations and use a suitable train.

## Plan the connection

1. **Find a source and destination.** Open the **Industry directory** and inspect the industries you want to serve. For
   freight, verify that one produces the cargo and the other accepts it. For passengers, choose towns with useful station
   locations.
2. **Choose station sites.** Place each station close enough to the industry or town to attract the cargo you want. Leave
   room for the track and a depot, and check construction previews before confirming.
3. **Connect the track.** Open **Railway construction** from the toolbar. Pick a rail-building tool and drag track between
   the station approaches. Keep the track pieces connected; use the preview to spot gaps and estimate the cost.
4. **Build rail stations.** Choose **Station along X** or **Station along Y**, then drag a rectangle where the platform
   should go. The station must connect to the railway and match the train route you plan to run.
5. **Build a rail depot.** Choose **Build rail depot** and place it beside your company's connected track. The depot
   connects its entrance to nearby company-owned rail when it is built.
6. **Buy a train.** Open the rail depot and choose **New Vehicles**. Buy a locomotive compatible with the track, then add
   wagons suited to your cargo—or passenger coaches for a town service. Check the purchase and running costs before
   buying.
7. **Make and assign a route.** Open **Trains** → **List of rail routes** → **New route**. Add the rail stations in the
   order the train should visit them and save. Open the train, choose **Assign a route**, select the new route, and choose
   **Assign this route**. Start the train from its vehicle window. See [Routes]({{ '/routes/' | relative_url }}) to learn how a shared plan can serve a fleet.

## How rail differs from roads

- Trains need a continuous railway and rail stations; road vehicles need connected roads and road stops.
- Rail construction, stations, depots, locomotives, and wagons can require a larger up-front budget. Check the live cost
  preview and vehicle catalog instead of assuming every route has the same price.
- A train's locomotive and wagons determine what it can carry. A coal train needs freight wagons that carry coal; a
  passenger service needs passenger capacity.
- Rail is often a good fit for long industry connections and bulk freight, but passengers can also travel between towns
  by train.
- Rail depots are for buying and managing trains; a road vehicle depot serves buses and trucks.

Check the station windows after construction. A well-connected line still needs compatible cargo, available station
capacity, and a destination that accepts the delivery.
